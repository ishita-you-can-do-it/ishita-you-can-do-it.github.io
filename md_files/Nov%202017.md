**Question and options (from the scanned paper):**


**Answer-key entry:** Option **2**.

#### Explanation and connected concepts: Personality structure: type, trait, system and structure

The supplied key gives option 2. The intended mapping is (a)-(ii), (b)-(i), (c)-(iv), (d)-(iii), following the terminology used by this question. A type is a broad category or cluster of dispositions; a trait is a relatively consistent tendency across situations; a system is an organised set of interacting personality components. The source's wording for 'structure' and 'trait' is not perfectly clean, so learn the conceptual distinction rather than memorising the wording alone. In Allport's trait approach, traits are enduring predispositions; cardinal traits dominate a life, central traits describe major characteristics, and secondary traits are narrower preferences or attitudes. Nomothetic research compares people on common dimensions; idiographic research studies the individual in depth.


### Question 2

**Question and options (from the scanned paper):**

```text
2.   Which of the following is true for Tip_of_the Tongue (TOT) state ?

     (a)   Feeling of Knowing                              (b)    Feeling that memory is available

     (c)   Retrievable memory                              (d)    Maintenance recoding

     Code :

     (1)   (a) and (b) only                                (2)    (a) and (c) only

     (3)   (b) and (d) only                                (4)    (c) and (d) only
```

**Answer-key entry:** Option **1**.

#### Explanation and connected concepts: Tip-of-the-tongue (TOT) phenomenon

Option 1: feeling of knowing and a strong feeling that the memory is available, although the target word cannot currently be retrieved. TOT is a temporary retrieval failure, often accompanied by partial phonological information (such as first letter or syllable). It is not proof that complete retrieval is guaranteed. Distinguish recognition (identifying a previously encountered item), recall (producing it without the item present), and feeling-of-knowing (metacognitive judgement that one could recognise or later retrieve it). Retrieval cues and context reinstatement may resolve TOT.


### Question 3

**Question and options (from the scanned paper):**

```text
3.   Read each of the following two statements - Assertion (A) and Reason (R); and indicate your answer
     using code given below :

     Assertion (A) : Conditioning done using partial reinforcement schedule decreases resistance to
                       change.

     Reason (R) :        Partial reinforcement schedules influence rate of responding.

     Code :

     (1)   Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).

     (2)   Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).

     (3)   (A) is true, but (R) is false.

     (4)   (A) is false, but (R) is true.
```

**Answer-key entry:** Option **4**.

#### Explanation and connected concepts: Partial reinforcement and resistance to change

The key marks option 4: the assertion is false and the reason true. Partial/intermittent reinforcement generally makes behaviour more resistant to extinction than continuous reinforcement; this is the partial-reinforcement extinction effect (PREE). The phrase 'resistance to change' is broader than resistance to extinction, so do not treat them as identical. Schedules: fixed ratio (reinforcement after a set number of responses), variable ratio (after an unpredictable number; high, steady responding), fixed interval (first response after a fixed time; scalloped pattern), variable interval (unpredictable time; steady responding). Partial schedules influence response rate and persistence.

### Question 6

**Question and options (from the scanned paper):**

```text
6.   Read each of the following two statements - Assertion (A) and Reason (R); and indicate your answer
     using code given below :

     Assertion (A) : Increase in synaptic strength is due to the simultaneous stimulation of pre - synaptic
                       and post - synaptic neurons.

     Reason (R) :         Long term Potentiation can be induced in the laboratory by stimulating the pre -
                          synaptic neurons with pulses of high frequency of electricity for few seconds.


     Code :

     (1)   Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).

     (2)   Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).

     (3)   (A) is true, but (R) is false.

     (4)   (A) is false, but (R) is true.
```

**Answer-key entry:** Option **1**.

#### Explanation and connected concepts: Hebbian learning and long-term potentiation (LTP)

Option 1 according to the key. Hebb's principle is commonly summarised as 'cells that fire together wire together': coordinated pre- and postsynaptic activity can strengthen synaptic efficacy. LTP is a persistent increase in synaptic strength after particular patterns of stimulation, classically high-frequency stimulation in hippocampal pathways. At many glutamatergic synapses, NMDA receptor activation permits calcium entry when glutamate binding coincides with postsynaptic depolarisation; intracellular cascades increase AMPA receptor function/number. LTP is a major model for synaptic plasticity and learning, but not the only mechanism of memory.


### Question 7

**Question and options (from the scanned paper):**

```text
7.   Concepts allow us to identify things without _________.

     (1) Learning               (2) Relearning              (3) Recognition              (4) Remembering
```

**Answer-key entry:** Option **2**.

#### Explanation and connected concepts: Concept formation and recognition without relearning

Option 2: concepts allow classification/identification without having to relearn every individual instance. A concept is a mental representation of a category, grouping objects, events or ideas by shared properties. Concepts reduce cognitive load, support inference and enable generalisation. Classical view: category membership is defined by necessary and sufficient features. Prototype view: members are judged by similarity to a typical example (Rosch). Exemplar view: comparison is made with stored examples. Hierarchical concepts include superordinate (animal), basic (dog) and subordinate (Labrador) levels.

```text
10.   Binocular cues depend on :
```

**Answer-key entry:** Option **1**.

#### Explanation and connected concepts: Binocular depth cues

Option 1: binocular cues depend on the coordinated use of both eyes, including the combination and difference between retinal images. ### Question 11

**Question and options (from the scanned paper):**

```text
11.   The correct order of the stages of Master’s and Johnson’s sexual response cycle is :

      (1)   plateau, excitement, orgasm, resolution

      (2)   excitement, plateau, orgasm, resolution

      (3)   excitement, orgasm, plateau, resolution

      (4)   excitement, plateau, orgasm, refractory
```

**Answer-key entry:** Option **2**.

#### Explanation and connected concepts: Masters and Johnson sexual response cycle

Option 2: excitement → plateau → orgasm → resolution. Masters and Johnson described four physiological phases. Excitement involves arousal and genital/vascular changes; plateau is sustained high arousal; orgasm involves rhythmic muscular contractions and peak release; resolution is return toward baseline. A refractory period, particularly prominent in many males, is not the fourth phase in the original four-stage model and varies greatly between individuals. Kaplan later proposed a desire–excitement–orgasm model, highlighting desire as a distinct phase.

```text
       (1) Frontal Lobe                                 (2) Occipital Lobe

       (3) Parietal Lobe                                (4) Temporal Lobe
```

**Answer-key entry:** Option **1**.


**Answer-key entry:** Option **1**.

## High-yield revision sheet

### Personality and assessment
- **Allport:** cardinal, central and secondary traits; idiographic emphasis; canonical definition refers to dynamic organisation of psychophysical systems.
- **Nomothetic vs idiographic:** group-level general laws and comparisons vs detailed understanding of an individual's uniqueness.
- **Eysenck:** Extraversion, Neuroticism and Psychoticism; EPQ-R also includes a Lie scale.
- **Big Five / NEO:** Neuroticism, Extraversion, Openness, Agreeableness, Conscientiousness.
- **MMPI-2 clinical scale sequence:** Hs–D–Hy–Pd–Mf–Pa–Pt–Sc–Ma–Si (1–0). Validity indicators must be considered before clinical interpretation.
- **Projective tests:** ambiguous stimuli; examples include Rorschach, TAT and Rosenzweig Picture-Frustration Study. Objective inventories use standardised items and scoring.
- **Ipsative scores:** compare a person's preferences within that person's profile; norm-referenced scores compare a person with a reference group.

### Learning, memory and cognition
- **Respondent/classical conditioning:** elicited responses; unconditioned and conditioned reflexes.
- **Operant conditioning:** emitted behaviour shaped by consequences; baseline operant level is measured before systematic reinforcement.
- **Partial reinforcement extinction effect:** intermittently reinforced behaviour is often more resistant to extinction.
- **Memory:** episodic = personal events; semantic = facts/meanings; procedural = skills; working memory actively maintains/manipulates information.
- **Forgetting:** encoding failure, retrieval failure, interference and consolidation problems. Encoding specificity concerns overlap between encoding and retrieval cues.
- **TOT:** feeling of knowing/availability with temporary failure to retrieve a specific item.
- **Concepts:** prototypes, exemplars and defining features support categorisation and generalisation.
- **Reasoning biases:** confirmation bias, framing, availability, representativeness, anchoring and belief perseverance.

### Biological, sensory and perceptual psychology
- **Afferent** pathways carry sensory information toward the CNS; **efferent** pathways carry motor commands away from it.
- **Sympathetic:** fight-or-flight; pupil dilation, increased heart rate, bronchodilation, reduced digestion, dry mouth.
- **Auditory ossicles:** malleus → incus → stapes; sound path from tympanic membrane into cochlea.
- **Auditory central pathway (simplified):** cochlear nuclei → superior olivary complex → inferior colliculus → medial geniculate nucleus → primary auditory cortex.
- **Depth cues:** binocular disparity and convergence; monocular cues include perspective, interposition, texture gradient, shading and motion parallax. Accommodation is an oculomotor/muscular cue.
- **Broca's area:** left inferior frontal region, commonly associated with nonfluent speech production; Wernicke-related posterior temporal regions are associated with comprehension.
- **LTP:** activity-dependent strengthening of synapses; NMDA-dependent calcium signalling is a key mechanism at many hippocampal glutamatergic synapses.

### Motivation, emotion and development
- **Motivation** energises and directs behaviour; distinguish needs, drives, motives and incentives.
- **James–Lange:** bodily response precedes/constitutes felt emotion; **Cannon–Bard:** emotion and bodily arousal occur simultaneously; **Schachter–Singer:** arousal plus cognitive label.
- **Masters–Johnson:** excitement → plateau → orgasm → resolution.
- **Freud's psychosexual stages:** oral → anal → phallic → latency → genital. Fixation claims are historical psychoanalytic theory, not modern diagnostic facts.
- **Yerkes–Dodson:** inverted-U relationship between arousal and performance; task difficulty changes the optimal level.

### Research methods and psychometrics
- **Independent variable:** manipulated factor; **dependent variable:** measured outcome; **controlled variable:** held constant/restricted; **organismic variable:** participant attribute; **stimulus variable:** property of presented material.
- **Random assignment** allocates participants to conditions; **random sampling** selects participants from a population. They serve different purposes.
- **CTT:** observed score \(X=T+E\); measurement error has expected value zero under the model. Reliability concerns consistency/true-score variance.
- **Deviation IQ:** \(IQ=100+15z\) on a common Wechsler metric; \(z=(X-\mu)/\sigma\). IQ 85 is \(z=-1\), approximately the 16th percentile.
- **Paired comparisons:** number of unique pairs \(n(n-1)/2\). For 9 items, \(9×8/2=36\).
- **Percentile rank** is the percentage of the norm group scoring at or below a given score; it is not percentage correct.

### Intelligence
- **Spearman:** g (general) + s (specific) factors.
- **Thurstone's seven primary mental abilities:** verbal comprehension, word fluency, number, spatial visualisation, associative memory, perceptual speed and inductive reasoning.
- **PASS / Cognitive Assessment System:** Planning, Attention, Simultaneous and Successive processing; associated with Das and Naglieri.
- **Fluid intelligence** involves reasoning in novel situations; **crystallised intelligence** reflects acquired knowledge and skills.
