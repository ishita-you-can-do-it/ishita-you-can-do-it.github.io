## High-yield topic-wise revision guide

### Learning, memory and cognition
- Latent learning can occur without immediate reinforcement; performance may appear later when motivation changes.
- Memory: decay explains forgetting through time-related weakening; interference explains competition from other learning.
- Deep semantic processing, elaboration and distinctive encoding generally improve retention.
- HAM represents knowledge through propositions and associative links; schemas and mental models guide interpretation.
- Gestalt grouping includes proximity, closure and continuity; depth cues include relative size, aerial perspective and convergence.
- Mnemonics include acronyms, clustering, imagery, pegword and method of loci.

### Biological and physiological psychology
- GABA and glycine are inhibitory neurotransmitters; dopamine and serotonin are modulatory transmitters.
- Raphe nuclei are serotonergic; myelin stains reveal pathways, while Nissl stains reveal neuronal cell bodies.
- Cataplexy is associated with narcolepsy; sleep apnoea involves breathing pauses; night terrors usually occur in NREM sleep.
- Rods recover sensitivity during dark adaptation; haptic memory briefly stores touch-related information.
- The lateral hypothalamus has historically been linked to feeding and ventromedial hypothalamic regions to satiety.

### Personality, motivation and counselling
- High achievement motivation is associated with moderate challenge, persistence, feedback and realistic goals.
- Hackman–Oldham's core job dimensions: skill variety, task identity, task significance, autonomy and feedback.
- Self-disclosure in counselling should be purposeful and client-centred; empathy requires avoiding projection.
- Rogers: unconditional positive regard supports congruence; conditions of worth can contribute to incongruence.
- Maslow's self-actualisers are described as accepting, reality-oriented and relatively comfortable with their limitations.
- Rollo May and Frankl emphasise existential awareness, freedom, responsibility and meaning.

### Social, organisational and developmental psychology
- Compliance: foot-in-the-door begins with a small request; door-in-the-face begins with a large request.
- Tuckman: forming → storming → norming → performing.
- Bandura's reciprocal determinism involves person, behaviour and environment.
- Attachment theories include psychoanalytic, learning, cognitive-developmental and ethological explanations.
- Super's career stages: growth → exploration → establishment → maintenance → decline.
- Organisational socialisation reduces newcomer uncertainty through induction, role clarity, feedback and mentoring.

### Research methods, statistics and psychometrics
- Independent-samples t-test compares two independent means; Mann–Whitney U is a non-parametric alternative.
- Multiple correlation: explained variance = R²; when R = .65, R² = .4225 or 42.25%.
- Matched-pairs t-test degrees of freedom = number of pairs − 1.
- Cattell's scree test uses the elbow; Kaiser–Guttman retains eigenvalues above 1.
- Varimax is orthogonal; Oblimin and Promax are oblique rotations.
- Second-order factor analysis examines correlations among first-order factors.
- Equal-appearing intervals scaling uses judges' placements; median scale values can be useful for skewed distributions.
