## Questions and explanations

### Question 1

**Question as printed:**

```text
Q.1 In what sequence the Social Combination rules are followed for group
success?

(1) Additive. disjunctive, compensatory. conjunctive
(2) Additive, compensatory, disjunctive, conjunctive
(8) Disjunctive. compensatory, conjunctive. additive
(4) Compensatory. conjunctive. disjunctive. Additive
```

**Printed answer key:** **1**.

#### Explanation and connected concepts

**Social combination rules:** Group tasks can be additive (members' contributions are combined), disjunctive (the best member's solution determines the outcome), compensatory (members' judgements are averaged or pooled), or conjunctive (the group must meet a minimum contribution/standard from every member). The item asks for the sequence in the paper's framework; use the printed key for exam practice.

### Question 5

**Question as printed:**

```text
Q.5 Which one of the following statement is true regarding the relationship of test
    length with reliability and validity'

     (1) As test length increases. only reliability improves.
     (2) As test length increases. only validity improves.
     (3) As test length increases. neither reliability nor validity improves.
     (4) As test length increases, both reliability and validity improve.
```

**Printed answer key:** **4**.

#### Explanation and connected concepts

**Test length, reliability and validity:** Increasing the number of good, representative items generally increases reliability by reducing measurement error (Spearman–Brown prophecy). ## High-yield topic-wise revision guide

### Cognitive psychology, memory and problem-solving
- Craik–Lockhart: shallow structural/phonological processing versus deep semantic processing.
- Atkinson–Shiffrin: sensory memory → attention → short-term memory → rehearsal/encoding → long-term memory.
- Schemas organise knowledge and influence memory; elaborative rehearsal links new information with existing knowledge.
- Means–ends analysis reduces the gap between current and goal states; heuristics are useful rules of thumb.
- Mnemonics include acronyms, categorical clustering, interactive imagery, pegword and method of loci.
- Memory systems: semantic, episodic, non-declarative and echoic; hippocampus supports new declarative memory consolidation.

### Biological psychology and health
- Resting potential depends on ion gradients, selective permeability and the sodium–potassium pump.
- Stress response: CRF → ACTH → glucocorticoids; prolonged stress can suppress immune function.
- Leptin signals energy stores and generally suppresses appetite; ghrelin stimulates hunger.
- EEG bands: delta, theta, alpha and beta; myelin insulates axon segments and speeds conduction.
- Alzheimer's pathology includes amyloid plaques and neurofibrillary tangles; Korsakoff syndrome is associated with thiamine deficiency.
- fMRI estimates activity using BOLD contrast; structural MRI images anatomy; PET uses radiotracers.

### Social, developmental and personality psychology
- Transactional Analysis ego states: Parent, Adult and Child; complementary transactions follow expected ego-state pathways.
- Idiosyncrasy credits allow a group member some deviation after earning status through prior contributions.
- Berkowitz's revision: frustration can produce negative affect and aggression cues, but aggression is not inevitable.
- Kohlberg: instrumental purpose → interpersonal cooperation → social-order maintenance → social contract → universal ethical principles.
- Erikson's ego strengths: hope, will, purpose, competence, fidelity, love, care and wisdom.
- Attribution biases include fundamental attribution error, actor–observer differences and self-serving bias.
- Pluralistic ignorance occurs when people infer from others' apparent calm that an ambiguous situation is not an emergency.

### Counselling, therapy and well-being
- REBT uses disputation, including imaginal disputation, to challenge irrational beliefs.
- Gestalt therapy emphasises present awareness and 'what/how' questions.
- Psychoanalytic resistance obstructs exploration of unconscious conflict.
- Empathy requires understanding the client's frame of reference without projecting one's own experiences.
- Eudaimonic well-being concerns virtue, flourishing and self-realisation; hedonic well-being focuses more on pleasure and comfort.

### Research methods, statistics and psychometrics
- Increasing test length with comparable items generally improves reliability; it does not guarantee improved validity.
- Point-biserial correlation is appropriate for a dichotomous item and continuous remainder score; phi is for two dichotomous variables.
- KR-20 is equivalent to Cronbach alpha for dichotomously scored items; split-half estimates depend on the split and correction.
- Spearman–Brown prophecy: \(r_{new}=\frac{nr}{1+(n-1)r}\).
- Kruskal–Wallis compares three or more independent groups on ranked/ordinal outcomes.
- MANOVA is useful for multiple correlated dependent variables; Pillai's Trace is often robust to assumption violations.
- In factorial designs, a significant interaction means the effect of one independent variable depends on the level of another.
- Quota sampling is non-probability sampling; individual selection probabilities cannot be specified.

### Indian and philosophical psychology
- The paper covers Vedantic states of consciousness, Sufi concepts of nafs, Mahayana Buddhism and Dilthey's human-science approach.
- Samkhya/Vedantic terminology and Sufi stages may vary by text and tradition; use the source's framing for this question set.
- Pragmatism emphasises practical consequences and useful inquiry; Dilthey emphasised understanding lived meaning rather than reducing human experience to positivist explanation.
