## Questions and explanations

### Question 1

**Question as printed:**

```text
1. When a target is defined by just one distinctive feature which is available on its feature map
   and it calls attention to itself is known as: -
   (A) Binding effect
   (B) Pop-out effect
   (C) Conjunction effect
   (D) Orienting effect
```

**Printed answer key:** **B**.

#### Explanation and connected concepts

**Feature integration and visual search:** A feature search is efficient when a target differs from distractors by one basic feature (such as colour or orientation). It appears to 'pop out' without serial inspection. Conjunction search requires combining two or more features and usually demands more focused attention.


### Question 2

**Question as printed:**

```text
2. The tendency to believe that a task will take less time than it really will, is known as: -
   (A) Magical thinking
   (B) Planning Fallacy
   (C) Optimistic Bias
   (D) Fundamental Attribution Error
```

**Printed answer key:** **B**.

#### Explanation and connected concepts

**Planning fallacy:** People underestimate the time, costs or effort required to complete a task, even when they have experience with similar tasks. It is distinct from general optimism because the bias concerns forecasts about one's own task completion.

## High-yield topic-wise revision guide

### Cognitive psychology, attention and memory
- Feature integration: single-feature targets can pop out; conjunction searches require focused attention.
- PRP is a central processing bottleneck when two stimuli arrive close together.
- Broca's area contributes to speech production and language processing; associative agnosia is a failure to connect a percept with meaning.
- False memories demonstrate the reconstructive nature of autobiographical memory and the effects of suggestion.
- Declarative memory includes episodic and semantic memory; working memory includes central executive, phonological loop, visuospatial sketchpad and episodic buffer.
- Encoding specificity concerns overlap between learning and retrieval cues; retrieval-induced forgetting reflects competition among memories.
- Metacognition includes monitoring, regulation and evaluation of thinking; LTP is a lasting increase in synaptic efficacy.

### Learning, motivation and emotion
- Skinner's verbal operants: mand, tact, echoic and autoclitic.
- Behaviourism: Watson's objective psychology, counterconditioning (Mary Cover Jones), and operant learning.
- Reinforcement schedules: fixed interval reinforces the first response after a set time interval.
- Emotion theories distinguish physiological arousal, cognitive appraisal and patterns of autonomic activity.
- Flow involves clear goals, immediate feedback, challenge-skill balance and absorption.
- Cognitive dissonance can be reduced by attitude change, adding consonant cognitions or reinterpreting behaviour.

### Personality, counselling and social psychology
- Rogers: openness, authenticity and caring are features of the fully functioning person.
- Reflection and empathy communicate understanding of a client's internal frame of reference.
- Cattell's 16PF emerged from factor-analytic work using L-, Q- and T-data.
- Nomothetic approaches seek general laws across people; Allport is associated with idiographic emphasis, Eysenck with nomothetic trait research.
- Compliance techniques: door-in-the-face and that's-not-all rely on reciprocity; foot-in-the-door relies on consistency.
- Diffusion of responsibility helps explain the bystander effect; realistic conflict theory links competition with prejudice.
- Satir's communication stances include congruent, placating, blaming and super-reasonable.

### Biological, health and developmental psychology
- Pheromones are environmental chemical signals; afferent pathways carry sensory information toward the CNS.
- Parkinson's disease is associated with motor symptoms and dopaminergic dysfunction; Huntington's is an inherited neurodegenerative condition.
- Biopsychosocial health models integrate biological, psychological and social influences and emphasise patient participation.
- Kohlberg's social-contract orientation is post-conventional; adolescent identity formation includes differentiation, exploration, rapprochement and consolidation in this paper's sequence.
- Primary, secondary and tertiary prevention refer respectively to preventing onset, early detection/treatment and limiting established disease consequences.

### Research methods, statistics and psychometrics
- Kruskal–Wallis is the non-parametric counterpart of one-way ANOVA for independent groups.
- MANOVA compares groups on multiple related dependent variables.
- Phi is appropriate for two dichotomous variables; point-biserial is appropriate for one dichotomous and one continuous variable.
- Item discrimination index: \(D=(U-L)/n\), where U and L are upper- and lower-group correct counts.
- Cronbach alpha estimates internal consistency; the Spearman–Brown formula predicts reliability after changing test length.
- KR-20/KR-21 apply to dichotomously scored items, even if each item offers several response alternatives.
- In a factorial ANOVA, an interaction hypothesis is tested using \(MS_{A\times B}/MSE\).
- Homogeneity of variance concerns similarity of group variances; a ratio of largest to smallest standard deviations near one is a rough diagnostic, not a formal test.

### Indian and philosophical psychology
- Samkhya distinguishes Purusha and Prakriti; the paper's yoga-cognition item uses manas, ahamkara and buddhi.
- Buddhist accounts connect craving with suffering and its cessation.
- The paper includes concepts from Sufism (nafs), Greek philosophy (eudaimonia) and Indian knowledge traditions (Turiyavastha, Aparavidya, Paravidya and Nididhyasana).
