## High-yield topic-wise revision guide

### Social and developmental psychology
- Altruism is helping without expected personal gain; prosocial behaviour is the broader category of actions intended to benefit others.
- Marcia's identity statuses: diffusion (no exploration/commitment), foreclosure (commitment without exploration), moratorium (exploration without commitment), achievement (exploration followed by commitment).
- Harlow's studies highlight contact comfort; Bandura's observational learning involves attention, retention, reproduction and motivation.
- Cultural context can influence attention and perception; social psychology studies social cognition, diversity and intergroup processes.
- Premack principle: a more probable/preferred activity can reinforce a less probable activity.

### Cognitive, learning and memory psychology
- Fluid intelligence supports novel reasoning; crystallized intelligence reflects acquired knowledge.
- Craik–Tulving levels: physical/structural → acoustic/phonological → semantic processing.
- Connectionist models represent cognition as patterns across interconnected units; schemas organise knowledge about the world.
- Signal Detection Theory separates sensitivity from response criterion/bias.
- Mnemonics: interactive imagery, acronyms, method of loci and pegword system.
- Emotion can strengthen some memory features, but vivid emotional memories are not guaranteed to be accurate.

### Biological and abnormal psychology
- XXY is associated with Klinefelter syndrome; the hypothalamus regulates pituitary/endocrine activity.
- The amygdala contributes to emotional learning and memory; lateral and ventromedial hypothalamic regions are classically linked to hunger and satiety.
- Depersonalisation concerns detachment from self; derealisation concerns unreality of the external environment.
- Echolalia is repetition of speech; REM Sleep Behaviour Disorder involves acting out dreams due to reduced REM atonia.
- CT uses X-rays; MRI uses magnetic resonance; PET uses radioactive tracers; fMRI uses blood-oxygen-level changes.

### Personality, motivation and organisational psychology
- Intrinsic motivation is driven by inherent interest; achievement motivation concerns standards of excellence; sensation seeking involves novelty/intensity.
- Self-determination theory highlights autonomy, competence and relatedness; controlling rewards can undermine autonomy.
- Hackman–Oldham dimensions: skill variety, task identity, task significance, autonomy and feedback.
- Equity theory concerns perceived fairness of input–outcome ratios; situational leadership varies task and relationship behaviour.
- Organisational behaviour addresses productivity, absenteeism, turnover, satisfaction and commitment.

### Research methods, statistics and psychometrics
- Multiple correlation R ranges from 0 to 1; the squared coefficient R² represents proportion of variance explained.
- For one factor with k levels, df = k − 1 and mean square = sum of squares / df.
- A 4×3×3 factorial design has one three-way interaction term.
- Time-series observations often show autocorrelation; ARIMA is one modelling approach.
- Scree plot: factor/component order on X-axis, eigenvalue on Y-axis. Common-factor extraction with retained unities is consistent with principal axis factoring.
- Orthogonal rotations (e.g. Varimax, Quartimax) keep factors uncorrelated; oblique rotations (e.g. Promax, Oblimin) allow correlations.
- Rotation does not change the total variance explained by the retained factor solution.
