### Question 1

**Question and options as printed:**

```text
1. Teplov while discussing the strong/weak nervous systems believed that the point of
·transmarginal inhibition' is the point belong which:
   (A) The magnitude of response decreases as stimulus intensity decreases
   (B) The magnitude of response increases as stimulus intensity decreases
   (C) The magnitude of response increases irrespective of stimulus intensity
       decrease/increase
   (D) The magnitude of response decreases irrespective of stimulus intensity
       decrease/increase
```

**Printed answer key:** **A**.

#### Explanation and connected concepts: Teplov and transmarginal inhibition

Teplov, working in the Pavlovian tradition, described nervous-system strength in terms of the capacity to tolerate intense or prolonged stimulation. At transmarginal inhibition, protective inhibition appears and the response can decline despite further increases in stimulus intensity. The key marks A; read the item as a threshold phenomenon rather than a simple linear stimulus–response rule.

## High-yield revision guide

### Biological psychology and psychopharmacology
- **Transmarginal inhibition:** protective inhibition at very high stimulation; associated with nervous-system strength/weakness in Pavlovian and Teplov traditions.
- **Hypovolemia:** reduced blood volume → reduced blood pressure; renin–angiotensin activation and thirst help restore fluid volume.
- **Glutamate:** major excitatory neurotransmitter; NMDA/AMPA receptors are central to many forms of synaptic plasticity and LTP.
- **Na⁺/K⁺ pump:** maintains ionic gradients needed for resting potential and action potentials. Neurons rely on glucose as a major energy substrate.
- **DBS:** implanted electrodes and pulse generator; **rTMS:** non-invasive magnetic stimulation; **ECT:** induced seizure under anaesthesia; **VNS:** vagus nerve stimulation.
- **ADH/vasopressin:** made in hypothalamus, stored/released by posterior pituitary; promotes renal water reabsorption.
- **Absolute refractory period:** sodium channels are inactivated; no second action potential can be initiated. In the relative refractory period, a stronger stimulus may work.
- **PET:** can use radiolabelled glucose analogues such as FDG to measure metabolic activity.

### Learning, attention and cognition
- **Classical conditioning:** backward (US before CS), simultaneous, delayed (CS overlaps US), trace (CS ends before US).
- **Operant reinforcement schedules:** fixed/variable ratio depend on response count; fixed/variable interval depend on elapsed time.
- **Insight learning:** exploration/inspection and focused attention precede restructuring and solution; distinguish insight from gradual trial-and-error learning.
- **Executive functions:** planning, inhibition, working memory and cognitive/set shifting; declarative memory is not itself usually classed as an executive function.
- **Metacognition:** knowledge about cognition and monitoring/regulation of one's thinking; models differ in how they label knowledge, experience, control and regulation.
- **Heuristics:** efficient mental shortcuts; lexicographic choice, satisficing and elimination by aspects are examples.
- **Implicit memory:** skills and dispositions can be retained without conscious recollection.
- **Vygotsky:** social interaction, cultural tools and language mediate development; private speech becomes internal/inner speech.

### Personality, counselling and positive psychology
- **Hardiness:** commitment, control and challenge.
- **Rogers:** actualising tendency, growth, congruence and constructive development; accurate symbolisation of experience is important.
- **Frankl:** creative, experiential and attitudinal values are routes to meaning.
- **Rollo May:** innocence → rebellion → ordinary consciousness of self → creative consciousness of self.
- **Sternberg's investment theory:** creative people pursue ideas with potential that are initially undervalued, then help establish their value.
- **VIA strengths:** 24 character strengths include creativity, forgiveness and prudence; resilience is important but is not one of the 24 named strengths in the standard VIA classification.
- **Happiness frameworks:** Diener—subjective well-being/life evaluation; Ryan & Deci—autonomy, competence, relatedness; Waterman—personal expressiveness; Aristotle—eudaimonia/flourishing.

### Social psychology and motivation
- **Door-in-the-face:** large request followed by smaller request; reciprocity/concession is the mechanism.
- **Exogenous attention:** stimulus-driven and involuntary; **endogenous attention:** goal-directed and voluntary.
- **Bandura's self-efficacy sources:** mastery/performance accomplishments, vicarious experience, verbal persuasion and physiological/emotional states.
- **Aggression:** general aggression intends harm; prosocial aggression supports accepted norms; antisocial aggression violates norms; sanctioned aggression is permitted by group norms.
- **Social identity theory:** self-concept partly derives from group membership. **Realistic group conflict:** competition over resources can produce prejudice. **Social dominance theory:** group hierarchies and discrimination.
- **Milgram:** obedience to authority; **Asch:** conformity; **Sherif:** autokinetic effect/realistic conflict studies; **Cialdini:** compliance principles.
- **Social facilitation:** presence/evaluation of others can increase arousal and strengthen dominant responses—helpful for well-learned tasks, harmful for unfamiliar/complex tasks.
- **Erikson:** identity vs role confusion precedes intimacy vs isolation; generativity vs stagnation follows it.

### Research methods, statistics and psychometrics
- **CTT:** observed score \(X=T+E\); under standard assumptions, true-score and error variance are uncorrelated and observed variance decomposes into true plus error variance.
- **Reliability:** test–retest (time stability), alternate forms (form equivalence), coefficient alpha (internal consistency), Cohen's kappa (inter-rater agreement).
- **Paired-samples t-test:** same participants measured twice; \(df=n-1\). For 60 paired participants, \(df=59\).
- **Regression:** \(R^2\) is the proportion of outcome variance explained by the predictor set. With \(N=200\) and \(k=4\), overall F degrees of freedom are \(df_1=4\), \(df_2=200-4-1=195\).
- **Dummy variable:** numeric coding for a categorical predictor (e.g., rural = 0, urban = 1).
- **Multicollinearity:** strong intercorrelations among predictors can inflate standard errors and make individual regression coefficients nonsignificant.
- **Homoscedasticity:** residual variance is approximately constant across fitted values/predictor levels; heteroscedasticity violates this assumption.
- **Normality:** Q–Q plots, Shapiro–Wilk and Jarque–Bera are used to assess univariate normality; scatterplots inspect relationships.
- **KMO:** sampling adequacy for factor analysis; higher values and smaller partial correlations are desirable. Values below .50 are generally inadequate.
- **Bartlett's test of sphericity:** significant result rejects the identity-correlation-matrix null, supporting factorability.
- **Kaiser–Guttman rule:** retain factors with eigenvalues greater than 1.
- **Cross-loading:** an item loads on multiple factors, creating ambiguity and potential discriminant-validity concerns.
- **Paired comparisons:** number of pairs is \(n(n-1)/2\), so the method becomes burdensome as the item pool grows.

### Philosophy, culture and Indian traditions
- **Ontology:** concerns existence and the nature of being; epistemology concerns knowledge.
- **Duhem–Quine thesis:** hypotheses are tested alongside auxiliary assumptions, complicating simple falsification.
- **Gestalt qualities:** organised wholes can have qualities not reducible to a sum of elementary sensations.
- **Integral Yoga and Indian philosophical terms:** preserve the question's tradition-specific terminology; meanings and sequences can vary across texts and schools, so avoid treating one exam matching scheme as universally exhaustive.
- **Sufism:** stations commonly include repentance, renunciation, poverty and annihilation of ego/self.

### Health and social indicators
- **Absolute poverty:** inability to meet a fixed minimum basic-needs standard; poverty gap measures the shortfall of poor people's income/consumption below the poverty line.
- **Total cholesterol estimate:** HDL + LDL + VLDL; VLDL is often approximated as triglycerides/5 in mg/dL, giving HDL + LDL + 20% of triglycerides. This estimate has clinical limitations.
- **Cancer terms:** leukaemia—blood-forming tissues; carcinoma—epithelial tissue; sarcoma—connective tissue; lymphoma—lymphatic tissue.
