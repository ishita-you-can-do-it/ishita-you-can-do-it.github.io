### Question 1

**Question and options as printed/extracted:**

```text
1. Mr. A approached the therapist for anger management. After Mr. A narrated an
  incident in which he experienced anger, which of the following is the most
  appropriate way of further questioning at the initial level?

1. Could you please tell me why you got angry?
2. Could you please explain what made you angry?
3. Please help me understand whom you got angry at.
4. Please explain how you felt when you were angry.
```

**Printed answer key:** **Option 2**.

#### Explanation and connected concepts: Initial counselling questions

At the initial stage, an open, non-leading question that invites the client to describe the trigger is preferable. 'What made you angry?' explores the antecedent without assuming whom the client blamed or prescribing an interpretation. Follow-up assessment can then explore thoughts, bodily sensations, feelings and behaviour.


### Question 2

**Question and options as printed/extracted:**

```text
2. Which of the following are true for bootstrapping?
(a) It involves a large number of subsamples.
(b) It does not rely on assumptions about the population.
(c) It is applicable to nonmetric data only.
(d) It is based on sample data only.

Select the correct option:
1. Only (a) and (b)
2. Only (a), (b) and (c)
3. Only (a), (b) and (d)
4. Only (b), (c) and (d)
```

**Printed answer key:** **Option 3**.

#### Explanation and connected concepts: Bootstrapping

Bootstrapping repeatedly draws samples (usually with replacement) from the observed sample to estimate a statistic's sampling distribution, standard error or confidence interval. It is data-driven and does not require a fully specified population distribution, although it is not assumption-free in every respect. It is not restricted to nonmetric data; it can be used with many kinds of statistics and variables.

## High-yield revision guide

### Counselling and positive psychology
- **Person-centred counselling (Rogers):** empathy, congruence and unconditional positive regard; clients should be able to communicate in their own way without judgement.
- **Counselling lead:** facilitation and direction should be responsive to the client's needs; silence alone is not necessarily a therapeutic lead.
- **Post-traumatic growth:** stronger relationships, personal strength, new possibilities, appreciation of life and existential/spiritual change.
- **Ryff's psychological well-being:** autonomy, environmental mastery, personal growth, positive relations, purpose in life and self-acceptance.
- **Ardelt's wisdom model:** cognitive, reflective and affective dimensions.
- **Savouring:** conscious attention to positive experience; **hedonic adaptation:** emotional effects of changed circumstances tend to fade.

### Biological psychology and neuroscience
- **Telencephalon:** cerebral hemispheres and major associated structures, including basal ganglia/corpus striatum.
- **Rhodopsin:** rod-cell photopigment for dim-light vision; a G-protein-coupled receptor.
- **Parathyroid hormone:** regulates blood calcium.
- **Multiple sclerosis:** immune-mediated CNS demyelination.
- **Huntington's disease:** HTT gene mutation; progressive movement, cognitive and psychiatric symptoms.
- **Pick's disease:** frontotemporal lobar degeneration.
- **Eye:** cornea/lens focus an inverted image on the retina; rods and cones transduce light.
- **Cutaneous receptors:** Meissner—light touch; Pacinian—deep pressure/vibration; free nerve endings—pain/temperature; Krause end bulbs historically linked with cold.

### Learning, memory and cognition
- **Implicit memory:** skills/habits shown without conscious recollection; **explicit memory:** conscious facts and events.
- **Episodic memory:** personally experienced events; **semantic memory:** facts/meanings; **procedural memory:** skills.
- **Bandura observational learning:** attention → retention → production/reproduction → motivation.
- **Tolman:** purposive behaviourism, cognitive maps, expectations and goal-directed behaviour.
- **Piaget:** assimilation fits new experience into existing schemas; accommodation changes schemas; operations are internalised logical actions.
- **Heuristics:** availability, representativeness and simulation; the simulation heuristic involves mentally imagining possible scenarios.
- **McGurk effect:** visual lip movements alter auditory speech perception.
- **Covert attention:** attention shifts without eye movement.

### Social psychology and organisational behaviour
- **Social identity theory:** self-concept partly derives from group memberships.
- **Outgroup homogeneity:** outgroup members are perceived as more similar to one another than ingroup members.
- **Evaluation apprehension:** concern about being observed/evaluated can alter performance.
- **Social facilitation:** presence of others can strengthen dominant responses.
- **Foot-in-the-door:** small request followed by larger request; **door-in-the-face:** large request followed by smaller request.
- **Bases of power:** reward, coercive, referent and legitimate.
- **Situational leadership:** telling = high task/low relationship; selling = high/high; participating = low task/high relationship; delegating = low/low.
- **False confessions:** coerced-compliant confessions occur while the person privately maintains innocence; coerced-internalised confessions involve doubting one's own memory or guilt.

### Personality and individual differences
- **Jung:** introversion/extraversion × thinking/feeling/sensation/intuition = eight basic types; archetypes belong to the collective unconscious.
- **Allport:** idiographic study of the unique individual; Cattell and Eysenck are associated more with nomothetic trait approaches.
- **Adler:** safeguarding strategies protect self-esteem and avoid confronting perceived inferiority/life tasks.
- **Cattell:** ergs, sentiments and attitudes/dynamic traits form an organised motivational network.
- **Autonomy:** self-determination and independence, a dimension of psychological well-being.

### Research methods, statistics and psychometrics
- **Bootstrapping:** repeated resampling with replacement from observed data to estimate sampling distributions and uncertainty.
- **Classical Test Theory:** observed score \(X=T+E\); under standard assumptions, true-score and error components are uncorrelated.
- **Item-remainder correlation:** point-biserial correlation is appropriate for a dichotomous item and a continuous remainder score.
- **Reliability:** Cronbach's alpha measures internal consistency; KR-20 is a special case for dichotomous items; split-half reliability depends on the split and correction.
- **Spearman–Brown prophecy formula:** estimates reliability after changing test length.
- **Standard error of measurement:** \(SEM=SD\sqrt{1-r_{xx}}\); an approximate 95% true-score interval is observed score ± \(1.96\times SEM\).
- **Deviation IQ:** mean 100, SD 15; percentile 84 is approximately z = +1, hence IQ ≈ 115.
- **Multiple regression:** adding a predictor cannot reduce unadjusted \(R^2\); adjusted \(R^2\) can decrease.
- **Factorial ANCOVA:** a 3 × 2 factorial design has six treatment combinations; a continuous covariate is included in the analysis.
- **Mean square:** \(MS=SS/df\). For a three-level factor, df = 2.
- **Residual df in a between-subjects factorial ANCOVA:** depends on sample size, number of cells and covariate parameters.

### Creativity
- **Four Ps:** person, process, product and press.
- **TTCT:** assesses creative thinking processes through dimensions such as fluency, flexibility, originality and elaboration.
- **Big-C:** eminent creativity; **Pro-c:** professional creativity; **little-c:** everyday creativity; **mini-c:** personally meaningful creativity in learning/experience.
- **Torrance's creatively handicapped:** high intelligence but low creativity.

### Social and developmental concepts
- **Binet:** mental competence increases during childhood; relative standing among same-age peers tends to remain comparatively stable.
- **Emotional development:** early happiness in familiar interaction, increasing anger/happiness, emergence of self-conscious emotions and later self-evaluation.
- **Empathy:** sensitive caregiver interaction supports emotional attunement and later empathic helping.
- **Autism spectrum disorder:** social-communication differences plus restricted/repetitive patterns of behaviour/interests.
- **Fetal alcohol spectrum disorders:** require prenatal alcohol exposure; social context may affect exposure risk and outcomes but is not the causal exposure itself.
