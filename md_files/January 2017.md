### Question 1

**Question as printed:**


**Printed answer key:** **1**.

## High-yield topic-wise revision guide

### Intelligence, assessment and psychometrics
- Guilford's SOI model: operations, contents and products. Products include units, classes, relations, systems, transformations and implications.
- Chronometric approaches use reaction time; non-verbal performance measures can be useful when verbal or literacy demands need to be minimised.
- Item analysis examines difficulty and discrimination. Internal consistency includes split-half, KR-20/KR-21 and Cronbach's alpha.
- T scores conventionally have mean 50 and SD 10, but this paper's study passage defines its normalized T scale as mean 100 and SD 20. Deviation IQ commonly has mean 100 and SD 15; always use the scale specified in the question.
- Phi is used for two binary variables; tetrachoric correlations are often considered for dichotomous items assumed to reflect underlying continuous traits.

### Learning, memory and cognition
- Proactive interference: old learning disrupts new learning. Retroactive interference: new learning disrupts earlier learning.
- Ebbinghaus' forgetting curve: steep early loss followed by a slower decline/plateau.
- Elaborative rehearsal links material to meaning and prior knowledge; retrieval cues support recall.
- Heuristics are shortcuts; algorithms are systematic procedures. Analogical reasoning transfers relational structure; deduction draws conclusions from premises.
- Gestalt principles include proximity, similarity, continuity, closure and figure–ground organisation.

### Biological and sensory psychology
- Rods support low-light vision and do not distinguish colour; cones support colour and fine detail.
- Auditory pathway: auditory nerve → cochlear nuclei → superior olivary complex → lateral lemniscus → inferior colliculus → medial geniculate nucleus → auditory cortex.
- The neural membrane is a phospholipid bilayer: hydrophilic heads face fluid; hydrophobic tails face inward.
- Linear perspective and convergence are monocular/binocular depth cues respectively; atmospheric perspective reduces distant detail.

### Motivation, emotion and personality
- Jung's shadow refers to less conscious/darker aspects of the personality; persona is the social mask; anima/animus are archetypal contrasexual images.
- Facial-feedback hypothesis links facial muscle feedback to emotional experience and is commonly associated with James–Lange theory.
- Approach–avoidance conflict occurs when the same goal has positive and negative valence.
- Bandura's moral disengagement mechanisms include moral justification, dehumanisation, displacement and diffusion of responsibility.
- Drive energises behaviour; needs reflect deficiency/imbalance; power motivation concerns influence over others.

### Research methods and design
- Laboratory experiments generally provide greater control over secondary variance than field experiments and ex-post-facto field studies.
- Naturalistic observation is useful in ethology but is not free from observer bias.
- Cross-sectional designs confound age and cohort; cohort effects such as the Flynn effect complicate developmental comparisons.
- Survey limitations can include sampling problems, social desirability and literacy/accessibility barriers; surveys can also be longitudinal.
